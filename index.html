How to read this
  • Priority (the default map) combines the day's hazard alert with the impact: who and what a landslide starting in a 250 m cell could reach. P1 = red alert with high or very high impact; P2 = red with medium, or orange with high or more; P3 = red with low, orange with medium, or yellow with high or more.
  • Hazard alerts come from a model of rain (forecast or observed) and terrain. Red is the top 1 % of all cell-days in the record, orange the top 3 %, yellow the top 10 %. Most days are green almost everywhere.
  • Day 1 is the day after the issue date, day 2 and day 3 the next two. The forecast gets less certain with each day.
  • Hindcast dates were run later with observed satellite rain (IMERG), not with the forecast issued that day.
  • The place list ranks places by probability x impact. Click a row to zoom to it. Click the map for the values of the most urgent 250 m cell in that ~1 km square.
  • Impact counts people in homes, road users, facilities, pilgrims in yatra months (May, June, September to November) and people cut off or on long detours if a lifeline road is blocked. It is an estimate from open data (OpenStreetMap, satellite building footprints and population grids) and misses things those miss.
  • Landslides that are never reported cannot be learned from, so the probabilities are a lower bound and quiet, remote areas may be under-rated.
